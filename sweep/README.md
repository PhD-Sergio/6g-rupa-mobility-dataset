# 6G-RUPA vs 5G under Mobility — Reproducibility Dataset

National, federation and non-terrestrial mobility sweeps for the paper *"One User
Plane to Rule Them All: From Handovers to a Network of Networks"*.

It contains the evidence base for the multi-country mobility evaluation: the real
radio-access fields (gNB locations from crowdsourced and official-registry sources),
the population and boundary inputs that place mobile agents, the four aggregated
result sweeps reported in the paper, and the per-shard raw output and logs behind
them.

## Layout

```
inputs/gnb/         Per-country, per-source gNB fields (license-bearing) — see PROVENANCE.md
inputs/population/  Municipality populations + administrative boundaries per country
results/            The four aggregated sweeps reported in the paper
results/raw/        Per-shard CSVs and run logs the aggregates were concatenated from
scripts/            Reproduction instructions (pointers into PLMN-GraphSim)
datapackage.json    Frictionless Data descriptor (schemas for every CSV)
```

`topology/` and `trajectories/` are intentionally absent. Both are rebuilt from
`inputs/` — the two-tier UPF/PSA deployment is produced by k-means
over the gNB field at load time, and trajectories are regenerated by
`main.jl trajectories` — so shipping them would add hundreds of MB of redundant
state that could drift out of step with the inputs.

"Source" is either the crowdsourced OpenCellID field or the country's official
registry where one exists.

Three countries carry a paired official registry (FCC ASR, ANFR, ISED) alongside the
crowdsourced field, which is what lets density and coverage-hole effects be separated
from the σ result: the registries are 2–3× sparser in station count than OpenCellID
in the same country, and the σ advantage moves by less than a point across the pair
(USA highway 67.9–68.7% OpenCellID vs 68.2% FCC ASR; France 67.3% vs 67.5%; Canada
67.6–68.0% vs 67.6–68.0%).

## National sweep (`results/national-sweep.csv`, 81 rows)

27 targets × 3 mobility profiles (pedestrian RWP 5 km/h, urban RWP 50 km/h, highway
Gauss–Markov 120 km/h), 1200 s, agents = population × 0.82 adoption / 1000 scale.

- **Core forwarding-state churn:** 5G grows with handover volume, up to 8.53 B writes
  (T-Mobile US, highway); 6G-RUPA is **0** in all 81 rows.
- **Per-handover signaling σ:** 6G-RUPA advantage **66.9–71.5%**, across a 64×
  handover-rate range (1.8–112.9 handovers/user/hr), a 45× edge-UPF range (18–817),
  six countries, three mobility profiles, and both crowdsourced and registry sources.
- **Anchor path-stretch (SSC-1):** intra-PLMN excess 0.0–5.1 km, confirming that
  anchor pinning is cheap inside a PLMN and only becomes material at the roaming
  boundary. Read as an order of magnitude, not per-row — ρ is the one column here that
  does not replicate across runs (see `scripts/reproduce.md`).

## Population scaling (`results/churn-data-verizon-1200.csv`, `churn-data-usa.csv`)

The O(n) claim above is a cross-sectional read of the national sweep. These two files
test it directly by holding the deployment fixed (Verizon US, 817 edge UPFs, 5 anchors,
highway Gauss–Markov 120 km/h) and sweeping represented population from 1 M to 274.7 M
active users.

- `churn-data-verizon-1200.csv` — full 1200 s runs, the headline figure. 5G core writes
  grow linearly with population, 19.0 M → 5.113 B; 6G-RUPA is 0 at every point.
- `churn-data-usa.csv` — earlier paired 300 s runs on Verizon OpenCellID and FCC ASR,
  retained as a source check: at 274.7 M users the two fields land within 1.8% of each
  other (1.390 B vs 1.415 B writes), which is the density-invariance argument in
  miniature. Not used by the headline figure.

## Anchor sensitivity (`results/anchor-sweep.csv`, 60 rows)

6 countries × anchor counts {2, 5, 10, 20, 40} × {pedestrian, highway}. Operators do
not publish anchor counts, so every other run in this dataset fixes the count with a
rule dominated by its own clamp (`round(pop / 10M)` bounded to `[2, 5]`). This sweep
exists to show the reported quantities do not depend on that choice.

- **σ advantage is invariant to the anchor count:** 66.84–68.64% across a 20× range in
  anchors, and `core_writes_6grupa` is 0 in every row. Both are set by the edge-UPF
  partition, not by the anchor tier — which is what makes the `[2, 5]` clamp harmless.
- **Anchor path-stretch ρ *rises* with anchor density**, from ≈0.0–1.5 km at 2 anchors
  to ≈0.5–4.9 km at 40 (excluding Portugal, see below). This is the opposite of the
  hypothesis stated in the run script, and it is the correct behaviour: 5G pins the
  session anchor at attach, so adding anchors improves the optimal reference faster
  than it improves the pinned one. ρ measures the penalty of pinning, and that penalty
  grows as the anchor set gets denser.

> **Known artifact — Portugal at 20 and 40 anchors.** Anchor placement clamps to
> `k = min(requested, #edge UPFs)`. Portugal has 18 edge UPFs, so its `psa=20` and
> `psa=40` rows both actually ran at 18 anchors; the `psa` column records the request,
> not the deployment. At that point every edge UPF is its own anchor, so
> `anchor_dist_opt_km` collapses to exactly 0 and ρ jumps to 44–53 km. Those four rows
> are a boundary artifact, not a trend point, and should not be read as ρ values. No
> other country reaches the clamp, and no row used elsewhere in the paper is affected
> (Portugal runs at 2 anchors everywhere else).

## Continental federations (`results/federation-continental.csv`, 60 rows)

Two independent federations, each spanning a contiguous landmass, grown by adding a
*country* before adding a second operator within one:

- `europe`: Movistar ES → MEO PT → Orange FR → Vodafone ES → NOS PT → SFR FR
- `north_america`: Verizon US → Telus CA → Telcel MX → AT&T US → Rogers CA → Movistar MX

K = 2..6 × both 5G handover semantics × three mobility profiles. The two federations
differ by an order of magnitude in area and in coverage gap, which is the point: the
per-crossing cost should not care.

- **Per-crossing σ:** RUPA advantage **86.15%** (`reestablish`) / **65.39%**
  (`ideal_ho`) — identical to two decimal places on both continents, flat in K and in
  mobility model.
- **Continuity:** under re-establishment, 5G session breaks scale with crossings, up to
  2.21 B scaled (europe) and 2.81 B (north_america); RUPA is **0** in all 60 rows.
- **Membership state:** 5G bilateral mesh grows O(K²) (1, 3, 6, 10, 15 agreements at
  K = 2..6); RUPA internetwork-DIF enrollments grow O(K) (2, 3, 4, 5, 6).
- **Roaming path-stretch:** 8.8–256.8 km excess, i.e. two orders of magnitude above the
  intra-PLMN figure — this is where anchor pinning actually costs something.

## Terrestrial–NTN (`results/ntn-sweep.csv`, 216 rows)

Starlink (550 km shell, SGP4 from `tles_starlink_550_sgp.txt`, 25° minimum elevation)
run against each of the nine national fields rather than against a single composed
Iberian field, because how often a user falls off terrestrial coverage is a property of
the country. 9 targets × terrestrial-range threshold r_terr ∈ {5, 10, 20, 30} km ×
{stationary, pedestrian, highway} × {Xn, N2} serving-satellite handover semantics,
50 000-agent cap, 1200 s, fixed seed 20260728.

- **Coverage holes are a source artifact as much as a geography fact:** at
  r_terr = 10 km, satellite-served share is 41.57% on Verizon OpenCellID but 7.75% on
  FCC ASR, and 0.73% on France OpenCellID vs 0.00% on ANFR. Crowdsourced fields
  overstate holes substantially, so any NTN claim needs the registry row beside it.
  Country ordering survives the source change: Canada 23.0–23.7%, Mexico 24.05%,
  Portugal 10.80%, Spain 3.27%, France ≈0%.
- **Stationary-network effect:** serving-satellite changes accumulate without user
  motion — 25 852 (Spain), 436 911 (Verizon US) satellite handovers over 1200 s at
  r_terr = 10 km with zero-speed users. The event is a property of the constellation,
  not of the user.
- **Per-event signaling:** terrestrial–NTN crossing advantage **86.15%**; serving-
  satellite handover advantage **82.61%** (N2 endpoint) / **66.67%** (Xn endpoint);
  moving-user terrestrial signaling stays at **66.80–68.44%**.
- **Continuity:** 5G boundary-crossing session breaks reach 153.6 M scaled sessions;
  6G-RUPA remains **0**. Serving-satellite changes are not charged as session breaks.
- **Measured outage is nonzero only in Canada** (0.193–0.287% of agent-time, in all 48
  Canadian rows, both sources, all ranges and profiles) — high-latitude gaps under a
  25° elevation mask. Every other country is at 0.

## Reproduce

**Read `scripts/reproduce.md` before comparing a rerun to these files.** Only `ntn_sweep`
is bit-reproducible; the other runs are unseeded, so a rerun lands within about 1% on the
headline quantities (σ advantage within 0.11 pp, handovers within 0.95%, core writes
within 1.07%, measured over ten full-scale replicate pairs) and does not reproduce ρ at
all. The aggregated CSVs are exact concatenations of the per-shard files in
`results/raw/`, which has been verified.

All four sweeps come from `PLMN-GraphSim` run scripts
dispatched through `main.jl <run>`; topologies, UPF/PSA placement and agent placement
are rebuilt from `inputs/` by that pipeline on every run — from an unseeded k-means, so
a rerun produces a different valid deployment rather than the same one.
`results/sweep-provenance.txt` records the exact simulator commit, branch, Julia
version and host that produced the shipped aggregates.
